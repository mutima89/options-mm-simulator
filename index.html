▼ What is an Options Market Maker?

A Market Maker (MM) provides two-sided quotes (bid & ask) for options, earning the bid-ask spread as compensation for taking on risk.

The MM's core challenge is managing the resulting inventory risk. They use delta-neutral hedging to neutralize directional exposure while profiting from spread capture, theta decay, and gamma scalping.

This simulator puts you in the MM's seat. Orders arrive continuously, and the system auto-hedges delta within a configurable tolerance.

▼ P&L Attribution — Where does profit come from?

An options market maker's P&L decomposes into four sources:

1. Spread Capture — The bid-ask spread on each fill. This is the compensation for liquidity provision.

2. Theta Decay — Options lose value over time. If the MM is net short options (negative gamma), they earn theta. If net long, they pay theta.

3. Gamma Scalping — When the MM hedges delta, they effectively buy low, sell high (long gamma) or sell low, buy high (short gamma). This is the convexity P&L.

4. Hedge P&L — The P&L from actual underlying share trades made to maintain delta neutrality.

Total P&L ≈ Spread + Theta + Gamma + Hedge + Residual

Residual includes cross-gamma effects and higher-order terms.

▼ Gamma / Theta Tradeoff

There is a fundamental tradeoff in options market making:

Long options = +Gamma (benefits from volatility) −Theta (pays time decay)

Short options = −Gamma (loses from volatility) +Theta (earns time decay)

In this simulator, observe how gamma drives the hedge rebalancing frequency. High gamma = delta changes faster = more frequent hedging. When gamma is negative (short options), delta moves against you as price moves, forcing costly rebalancing.

▼ Delta-Neutral Hedging — How It Works

Step 1: Calculate the portfolio's net delta = sum of (position Δ × position quantity).

Step 2: If |net delta| exceeds the hedge threshold, buy or sell shares of the underlying to bring exposure back to zero.

Step 3: As the underlying price moves, gamma changes delta, requiring continuous rebalancing.

The formula for a hedge: Hedge Qty = −Net Delta

With a threshold of ±0.10, the MM tolerates up to 0.10 shares of delta exposure before hedging, reducing transaction costs at the expense of small residual risk.

▼ Black-Scholes Model

Greeks are computed using the Black-Scholes pricing model:

d1 = [ln(S/K) + (r + σ²/2) T] / (σ√T)

d2 = d1 − σ√T

Call price: S · N(d1) − K · e−rT · N(d2)

Put price: K · e−rT · N(−d2) − S · N(−d1)

Where N() = cumulative normal distribution, S = spot price, K = strike, T = time to expiry (years), r = risk-free rate, σ = volatility.
